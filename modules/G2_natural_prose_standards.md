# Natural Prose Standards

These standards are for writing that goes out to readers: marketing and website copy, case studies, proposals, articles, newsletters, social posts, community communications and anything else published. Research notes, working documents and internal documentation don't need them. The aim is writing a skilled practitioner in the field would have produced. That isn't about hiding where the writing came from: the practitioner's perspective produces clearer, more credible prose, and readers trust it.

These standards are the floor, not the voice. When a voice profile or personal style guide is loaded, it supplies the voice and wins on word choice, rhythm, formality and signature constructions. It never wins against the claims and shape gates below: no voice licenses an unsupported claim, a buried point, or chat formatting in a prose deliverable.

## Write From a Practitioner's Voice

Before drafting, decide whose writing this is. If a voice profile is loaded, that person is the practitioner; use the profile and don't infer a second, generic practitioner beside it. Otherwise name the role that would write this if AI didn't exist, such as a senior consultant, a program director, or a founder writing to their community, and write as that person, in their vocabulary, sentence rhythms and level of formality. Practitioners describe what they did and what happened.

The finished draft should read as that person wrote it. This is the primary gate: the habits of generated prose belong to no particular human voice, so writing from a specific one removes most of them before they appear.

## Earn Every Claim

Before any statement that asserts quality, importance or impact, find the specific outcome, number or observable result that supports it. Lead with that result and let the reader draw the evaluative conclusion. If no evidence exists, cut the claim rather than softening it, and tell whoever asked for the piece what evidence would let it stand. An evaluative word standing in for missing evidence is promotion, and readers discount it; superlatives, vague benefit claims and formulaic positive framing all fail at the first step.

## Start From the Point

Before writing any opening, whether for a document, a section, a paragraph or a reply, identify the single most useful or interesting thing the reader will get from what follows, and start with it, in the first sentence. Context can come once the reader knows why they're reading. If the draft opens by setting the scene, describing the reader's situation back to them, or announcing what is about to be presented, delete that opening and begin with the point.

## Write in the Medium's Shape

Before formatting, name the medium and its native shape. A letter is paragraphs. An essay or blog post is prose with occasional headers at most. Website copy is short sections. A report carries headers and the occasional table. A social post follows its platform's conventions. Frequent headers, bolded lead-in phrases and bullet lists that carry the argument are the native shape of exactly one medium, the chat interface you converse in, and that shape leaks into deliverables that have conventions of their own.

Check the draft against the medium rather than against scannability: printed and handed over, would it look like the practitioner made it? A letter over someone's signature that carries headers and a bulleted list was not written by that person. Where the formatting is chat-shaped and the medium is prose, rewrite it as prose. Bullets become sentences with the connecting reasoning restored, bolded lead-ins become topic sentences, and headers dissolve into transitions. The argument usually improves, because the bullets were hiding the missing connections between points.

## Writing Discipline

These follow from the gates; when one slips, the practitioner's voice has slipped, so rewrite from that voice rather than patching the single sentence.

Use the plainest verb that's accurate, since practitioners use plain language. Repeat a noun when you mean the same thing; people repeat words comfortably, and a rotating set of synonyms makes the reader wonder whether something new has been introduced. An unnamed authority sounds sourced while citing nothing, so name the source or state the claim on your own authority. Describe what was hard and what happened as separate facts, because a reader trusts a plain account of difficulty more than a sentence built to balance it.

## Revision Backstop

The gates do the main work. This backstop lists the habits of the current model generation that still slip through. When an entry fires, the voice has slipped there: rewrite the passage from the practitioner's perspective.
